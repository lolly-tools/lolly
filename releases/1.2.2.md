# Lolly 1.2.2: the fast focused flippy one

Released 23 November 2026 · @Andy Fitz

These notes cover 67 pull requests and no direct commits, merged to `main` after [v1.2.1](https://github.com/lolly-tools/lolly/releases/tag/v1.2.1) up to 23 November 2026. One of them, #1093, is a batch of six changes and sixteen dependency updates. What stayed open is listed under "Not in this release". The engine moves from 1.348.0 to 1.352.0, and the tool-author core stays at 1.8.0.

Lolly 1.2.2 is the fast focused flippy one. Fast, because there is less to wait for: the terminal starts in a tenth of a second and writes more formats with no browser, and a tool opens with the card you pressed and one loading card in place of a blank screen. Focused, because the app has less to read and fewer variations: labels that repeated a heading are gone, tool and page descriptions fit two lines, and there is one close button, one segmented control and one popover. Flippy, because Lolly now lays out for the iPhone Duo and keeps its place when the phone flips shut and open again: the same layout, the same framing of the canvas and the same panels. The iPhone and iPad app, new on the App Store with 1.2.1, also gets its first round of fixes from real devices.

## Highlights

- **The terminal starts fast.** `lolly --version` answers in 0.10 seconds, down from 0.83.
- **More formats with no browser.** Captions (SRT, VTT, TTML, SCC) and palettes (CSS, SCSS, GPL) are written by the CLI, the TUI and the MCP server without one, and so is a Design page's PNG where no browser is available.
- **A picture drawn with no browser matches the browser, or the job says why not.** The browser-free route draws only what it was measured to draw the same way.
- **A tool opens with a sign that something is happening:** the pressed card, then one loading card on a plain ground, then the tool.
- **iPhone Duo.** Controls keep clear of the system's side column, and folding and unfolding returns the layout you had, panels included.
- **iPhone fixes from real devices:** controls no longer sit under the status bar, and the page no longer comes to rest pulled down.
- **Icons for a dark ground.** Assets offers each colourway's icon pairing for a dark surface, with the Dark ground switch beside the colours, in the grid and in an asset's own view.
- **One close button, one segmented control, one popover** across the app.
- **Less text in the interface.** Labels over headings are gone, hints that repeated their neighbour are gone, and 33 tool descriptions fit two lines.
- **A short About page** and a new page, **Lolly without AI**.
- **A component library that opens as an index**, four screens long in place of 54.
- **A refreshed comparison** on the positioning page, with the evidence for every score one press away.
- **Basses that talk.** Dubstep, brostep, drum and bass and electro get new synthesised bass voices measured against produced tracks, with two new bass instruments.
- **New genres:** Italo disco and Balearic, and Lofi and Drum and bass each become one genre with flavours.
- **Markdown into Design.** Import a Markdown deck and each slide lands on one of your design system's slide layouts.
- **Meeting Planner moves into Timezone**, the first of a run of merges towards fewer, stronger tools.

## Faster

- **The CLI starts in a tenth of a second.** Node's compile cache is on before the engine loads, and `--version` and `--help` load neither the engine nor the renderer. Measured on an M4 with the cache warm: `lolly --version` 0.83 s to 0.10 s, `lolly list` 0.63 s to 0.35 s, `lolly qr-code --output=qr.png` 1.09 s to 0.73 s. The first run after an update fills the cache and is slower. `LOLLY_COMPILE_CACHE=0` turns the cache off (#1029).
- **Captions and palettes with no browser.** `srt`, `vtt`, `ttml`, `scc`, `css`, `scss` and `gpl` are written straight from the engine in the CLI and the TUI. Before, `lolly color-palette --export=css` went to a headless browser and failed where none was built (#1029). The MCP server and the public `GET /tool/<id>.<ext>` route write the same seven, where the route used to answer 400 (#1038).
- **A Design page's PNG with no browser.** Where no browser tier is available the CLI and the TUI draw an admitted Design page from its drawing operations, and say so on stderr. With a browser available, the browser still draws the page. `LOLLY_DESIGN_RASTER=resvg` asks for the browser-free route (#1032).
- **Opening a tool.** The card you press dims in the first frame after the press. The gallery then leaves and one loading card shows on the plain ground until the tool is ready, so the screen is never empty. A tool that is ready within a tenth of a second comes straight in with no card. Back or Escape while a tool loads returns to where you were (#1046).
- **A faster start in the browser.** The app paints before the server probe answers, and a second visit reuses the first. On a throttled phone profile the gallery is interactive in about 1.3 s cold and under half a second on a second visit (#1053, through #1079).
- **Projects with many sessions.** Gallery and Projects pictures are kept by reference and the session list is lean: listing 1,000 sessions took 59.5 ms and now takes 4.7 ms, and the first Projects tile shows in about half the time (#1079, engine 1.351.0).
- **A quieter gallery.** A gallery visit reads the looks that are already finished and draws a missing one only when the device can afford the work: the card is on or near the screen, the page is at rest, and the device is not asking for less (Save-Data, a low battery, little memory). Thumbnail copies are made in a worker. No picture changes; work is put off, moved off the main thread or not done twice (#1112).
- **Smaller packs.** The Lolly Demo and Lolly Starter packs lose 306 pre-rendered example files and two bundles that nothing had read since September, about 9.5 MB (#1031). The SUSE pack follows (#1054).

## Pictures that match the browser

- **The browser-free route draws only what it was measured to draw.** Compared with the browser, the route drew 39 of 57 measured cases outside the fidelity bands. It now refuses those, each with a sentence saying why, and hands the job to the browser tier. Where there is no browser tier the job fails with exit code 3, prints the reason and writes nothing (#1037).
- **Behaviour change.** Several tools that drew a PNG with no browser now need the browser tier, or the new `--allow-browser-free` flag, which accepts a browser-free picture that may differ. Asset Export, Gradient, Logo Wall, QR Code, Signature and Wordmark are admitted as they are and need neither.
- **PNG metadata matches.** A browser-free PNG carries the same colour profile, XMP, text and resolution chunks as the browser's, in the same order (#1037).
- **Vector pictures keep their exact size.** A picture's size was rounded to whole pixels before placing, so the Lolly logo sat 2 px narrow in a contained box. Gradient stops are written to full precision, where a stop at 12.5% went out at 13% (#1035).
- **CLI links say what they ignore.** A link carrying `kiosk`, `template` or `preset` used to render as if the parameter were absent. The CLI now warns, and `--strict` exits 2 (#1034).

## iPhone, iPad and iPhone Duo

- **Controls under the status bar.** The back button, the music generator's genre picker and other controls at the top of a tool no longer sit under the clock and the battery. The Lolly mark in a tool is no longer clipped (#1039).
- **The page pulled down.** After some navigations the app's page came to rest below the top of the screen, which showed as a black band in Dark appearance, and stayed that way across views. The app now puts the page back after each navigation and when it returns to the foreground (#1043).
- **Stylesheet order in the apps.** A view's styles could load ahead of the app's own in the Mac and iPhone apps, which changed which rule won. The order is now fixed in the page itself (#1039). The Mac app takes this in its next build.
- **iPhone Duo, side column.** On both of the Duo's screens the system keeps a column on the right. The profile menu, the gallery's controls, the stage's navigation, sheets and panels now keep clear of that column. Design's tool rail stays on the screen edge (#1052).
- **iPhone Duo, fold and unfold.** Open, fold, unfold used to return a different layout from the one the app started with, until relaunch: the inspector on the wrong side with a floating rail, or two panes with no tab header. The layout is now decided on every size signal at once. A canvas at Fit is framed again when the screen changes, and a zoom you set is kept. This also fixes a tabbed side column drawn at half height after a desktop window resize (#1058).
- **iPad: Home under the status bar.** In an ordinary tool such as Chart, the Home button, undo and redo sat under the iPad's clock, where a tap did not reach them. The sidebar's first row now starts below the status bar (#1110).
- **Assets filter row.** On a wide screen with a system inset, such as the open iPhone Duo, the filter row slid under the top bar once the page scrolled. It now pins below the top bar at every size (#1109).
- **The model download offer** no longer carries the label "Lolly for desktop" over its heading, which was wrong on a phone or tablet (#1111).
- **A gated instance in the app.** The iPhone, iPad and Mac apps refused the tools of any Lolly Work instance whose catalog was not signed with the key the app was built with, so an organisation's own instance connected and then showed nothing. The connect sheet now shows the key the instance signs with, and choosing the instance accepts that one key for that instance (#1088, through #1093). People already connected reconnect once.
- **Signed in for longer.** The app now keeps the session the server renews, where before a sign-in ended after the first session ran out, twelve hours by default (#1089, through #1093).
- **Panels come back after the phone layout.** The phone layout shows the inspector or the layers list, not both. Whichever it closed returns when there is room again, behind the panel you last brought forward. A panel you put down to its peek on the phone returns too. A panel you opened while folded is kept (#1065, through #1070).

## Music

Every sound is still synthesised on your device. Reference recordings were measured only; nothing from them is copied or sampled.

- **Bass.** The growl and the wobble now play a rough source through a mouth of fixed vowel peaks that crossfade, with an air band, where they used one swept band. Against produced bass, the energy from 8 to 16 kHz moves from 36 to 48 dB below the whole to 17 to 28 dB below (the references sit at 14 to 26). The reese gains a tear near 2.4 kHz (#1075).
- **Two new bass instruments:** Foghorn, a long flat held note that stops dead, and Jump-up, a short mid-range bark that can stutter. Drum and bass answers its reese with either (#1075).
- **Italo disco and Balearic** are new genres, with pages and signed samples (#1075).
- **Lofi is one genre with flavours:** Warm, Late night and Rainy, and seven new ones: Focus, Drift, Slow jam, Retro bounce, Bookstore, Zen and Cinematic. Drum and bass works the same way: Rolling, Bouncy, Dark, Soaring, Deep and Rave (#1075).
- **Sherbert** is brighter and wider after a new finish stage, and its tempo range widens to 130 to 160 (#1075).
- **Not in 1.2.2, still open:** chiptune has busier arpeggios in its drops (four figures, swapped in the second drop) and a rebalanced mix: the arpeggio was nearly inaudible under the drums. Synthwave and Sugar are rebalanced against the references too (#1076).
- **The Neurospicy player** shows the generated stations as a grid of genre tiles, and the speaker button opens one controls popover. Shuffle genres moves to another genre when a tune ends (#1075).
- **Saved tunes change.** Every dubstep, brostep, drum and bass and electro tune sounds different, and Sherbert, chiptune, synthwave and Sugar tunes are louder and rebalanced.

## Tools

The aim agreed for the months ahead is fewer, stronger tools: a tool that is really a mode of another moves into that tool, and its old links keep working.

- **Meeting Planner is now part of Timezone.** Timezone gains **Mark early and late hours** (amber before 08:00 and from 20:00, red before 06:00 and from 22:00, with the word on the card), and its reference clock takes a city as well as a zone name. Meeting Planner leaves the gallery; an old link or saved document still opens it, with a line that offers **Open in Timezone** and carries the title, people, cities and meeting time across (#1073).
- **A bare city name means the largest city of that name** in Timezone. London is London, GB without the country. Names shared by cities of similar size still ask (#1073).
- **A retired tool says where its work went.** Any tool that moves into another shows the same line at the head of its controls (#1073).
- **Flow Chart is now part of Design.** Flow Chart leaves the gallery. An old link or saved chart still opens, with **Open in Design**, which carries the cards, their pictures and initials, the loose layers and the connectors across. Design has a Flow Chart template to start from, and its connectors now land on their boxes (#1087, through #1093).
- **Tables in Markdown.** The `markdown` helper that tools share reads tables written with pipes, with left, centre and right columns. Booklet draws them (#1085, through #1093, engine 1.352.0).
- **A Markdown deck comes into Design as slides.** Design's Import panel takes a `.md` file. Each slide takes a layout of the design system's slide master by what it holds: a title, a heading with a list, a table, a quotation or a picture. Bold, italic, list levels and speaker notes travel as written. Markdown Slides stays in the gallery (#1077).

## Assets

- **Icon colours for a dark ground.** Each colourway has an icon pairing for a light ground and one for a dark ground. Assets offered only the first. The Colours row now offers the pairing for the ground the assets sit on, and a chosen colourway moves with the ground (#1019).
- **The Dark ground switch in an asset's own view**, in the Colours row beside the pairs it changes. The grid, the sheet and every switch move together (#1047).

## Design systems

- **Lolly Demo is always available.** An edit of the system an app or site came with makes a copy you own, named "Lolly Demo copy", and the original stays in the list unchanged. Before, the copy took the name and the original was hidden behind it.
- **An earlier copy is never written over.** A second round of edits started from the original used to replace the first copy. The earlier copy is now kept as a system of its own.
- **Connecting to an instance keeps the app's own system.** Its colours and type are copied onto the device once, so it is still there beside the instance's design system. Its logos are not carried.
- **On a device with an earlier edit**, the original is shown again, once. Hide it afterwards and it stays hidden.

## Design

- **The inspector's Selection and Canvas switch is a pill**, the same segmented control the rest of the app uses. Every input in the inspector has one corner radius, and the Size row's label lines up with the others (#1061, through #1070).

## One of each

- **One close button.** The app drew a cross about a hundred times in three hand-written ways, each with its own size, colour and hover, and several with no focus ring. There is now one, in two sizes, and every close button has a visible focus ring (#1028).
- **One segmented control** wiring behind every two-or-three-way switch (#1028).
- **One popover.** The export format picker and the emoji picker open through the shared popover. Both now close when you change view, the format picker is no longer clipped by the sheet, and an emoji picker inside a dialog opens above the dialog's backdrop (#1062).
- **Shared helpers** replace eleven copies of three small pieces of component code. Nothing a person sees changes (#1020).

## Less to read

- **No labels over headings.** Small capital labels above a heading are gone from Design System Setup, the brand download sheet, the rule editor, the Convert workbench and Prepare for sharing, and from the docs' format, convert and music pages, where a trail you can click replaces them (#1051, #1041).
- **Hints that repeated their neighbour are gone**, and Prepare for sharing's limits and storage notes open from one help icon beside Sources (#1051).
- **Export dialog.** The Protection fold is now **Content claims**, and the Design system card is its first card. The record is still on by default (#1045).
- **Tool descriptions.** 33 descriptions are cut to two lines on a gallery card, by deletion only: every word left was in the old text, in the same order (#1044).
- **Page descriptions.** 42 docs page descriptions are cut to 160 characters the same way (#1064).

## Guides

- **A short About page.** The About page, which is also the repository README, goes from 3,223 words to 584: what Lolly is, how it works in three steps, what you can make, making it yours and running your own copy. The list of every tool moves to `TOOLS.md` (#1026).
- **Lolly without AI.** A new page for a reader who wants no generated content in their files. Its first sentence gives the answer: Lolly does not write, draw or compose your work with a generative model (#1027).
- **Breadcrumbs** on docs pages never repeat the page's own title (#1026).
- **A horizon divider** replaces the hard rules in the docs: a hairline strongest at its centre that fades at both ends, with a soft glow in the design system's accent (#1033).
- **Plain writing.** 134 pages lose about 10,900 words, 3% of the total. Headings, tables, code, commands, links and numbers are unchanged, and every sentence that held a fact was kept (#1050).
- **Positioning.** The comparison's scores are refreshed as of 21 November 2026, its rows have plain names, and a **?** on each row opens what the row means and the evidence for every product, as a card over the table. Overall: Lolly 86, Penpot 70, Canva 61, Adobe 59, Brand DAM 45, Figma 43, Cloudinary 41, Render APIs 39 (#1055, #1059).
- **Plain writing, second part.** The remaining 73 pages had the same light pass, losing up to 4% of their words (#1069, #1056).
- **Links in the docs sheet work in Safari.** On an iPhone, tapping a section, a heading or a page in the docs page bar's sheet closed the sheet and went nowhere. Safari moves focus to the page when a link is pressed, and the sheet took that as focus leaving (#1072).
- **Install page.** Lolly for iPhone and iPad links to the App Store, the Mac download is 1.2.1, and the web app is given as the current Linux version (#1023, #1030, #1048).

## Component library

- **The page opens as an index.** Each family is a fold that starts shut, so the page is four screens long at 1440 wide where it was 54. A search opens the families that hold a match (#1021).
- **The part leads each card.** The stage is as tall as its part, and the reference and the `.penpot` download share one quiet line at the foot (#1021).
- **"Use this component" gives a line that works.** 48 cards printed an import line that held no function. Each line is now held to a real export by a test (#1021).
- **Plain headings** replace five labels over slogans (#1041).

## For agents and automation

- **`lolly-render`, a Rust crate that draws a Design page** from its drawing operations, on the CPU with pinned decoders, faithful or refused: a page is drawn whole or refused whole with a reason. Of 64 single-frame pages in the plan's corpus, 48 are drawn and 47 sit inside every release band against the browser tier. Nothing links the crate yet; no shell or render route uses it (#1113).
- **MCP previews.** `lolly://tool/{id}/preview` reads a tool's own preview in SVG, WebP or PNG, where it used to depend on files that are now removed (#1031).
- **Text formats on the public render route** carry the same sandbox and no-sniff headers as HTML and SVG, with tests that send hostile input to each of the seven (#1038).

## Languages

- **664 new interface strings in 26 languages**, 17,264 translations in all, covering every string added since the last wave (#1074).
- **Eleven strings that showed in English** in every language were stored under a key the app never looked up (#1080).
- **The shortened tool descriptions** are translated in 26 languages (#1082).
- **36 guide pages retranslated in 26 languages**, 936 files, so the translated guides match the English after the plain-writing pass. No native speaker has reviewed them (#1078).

## Safety and reliability

- **Trusted signers.** The C2PA trust list takes eight anchors from the official list, which changes which signers Verify reads as trusted (#1068, through #1079, engine 1.350.0).
- **The `url` crate** moves to 2.5.9 in the Mac and iPhone apps for RUSTSEC-2026-0592, a stack overflow on nested `blob:` addresses (#1093).
- **A gated origin stays gated.** The gallery's background drawing never runs under a sign-in gate (#1112).
- **Steadier tests.** The session-picture tests wait for the work itself and no longer for a count of turns, and a card-size test that failed on unrelated runs is fixed (#1108).
- **JPEG XL.** libjxl moves from 0.12.0 to 0.13.0, which takes Brotli 1.2.1 and its fix for CVE-2026-111497, an out-of-bounds write in the decoder that untrusted files could reach here. The codec runs as WebAssembly in a disposable worker (#1067).
- **Tracker modules.** libopenmpt moves from 0.8.7 to 0.8.10, with fixes for heap writes when loading crafted modules. Seven catalog modules render to identical audio before and after (#1067).
- **Camera raw.** Three fixes bound the time and memory a crafted file can take. No decoded pixel changes (#1067).

- **Dependencies.** Tauri moves to 2.12.7 with its API and CLI packages in both apps, along with zbus, plist, notify, the notification plugin, skrifa and the workflow actions (#1060, #1093). Left out on purpose, and closed: the font subsetter `skera` 0.10.0 with `write-fonts` 0.56.0, `fax` 0.3.0 and `hayro-ccitt` 0.5.0, none of which compile without a code change; `@cto.af/linebreak` 5.0.0, which fails one Unicode 17 line-break case; and `mediabunny` 1.66, which needs a code change and changes exported media.
- **CLI pack.** The published CLI's range for `onnxruntime-node` includes the version its speech library pins (#1022).

## Known limits

- **Not seen on a physical iPhone Duo.** The Duo work was done in the simulator, with Chromium and WebKit test browsers for the fold. Android foldables were checked with simulated fold reports.
- **The page pulled down on iPhone.** The cause was not found. The fix puts the page back, and the fault has not been seen since on the test iPhone.
- **Sequence** is not reopened after a visit to the phone layout.
- **Quit while folded at the peek** and the next launch opens with that panel shut.
- **Some new wording is English** in the translated interfaces: what was added after the translation wave of 21 November, such as Timezone's hour marks, the moved-tool line and the Markdown import. The 36 curated guide pages are retranslated; the engineering references stay English, as before.
- **Music was not judged by ear** before these notes: the changes were measured against references.
- **Markdown into Design:** a table arrives as text layers, a picture comes across only from a web address, and Markdown Slides' JSON spec is not read. Not checked on a phone.
- **Not carried from Meeting Planner to Timezone:** three map projections, pin shape and size, land and ocean colours, layout presets and zoom. Timezone finds cities in its offline list, so a small town may need its timezone typed in.
- **A kept Lolly Demo has no logos while connected to an instance,** and a device with an earlier in-place edit shows two rows named Lolly Demo, one marked "Edited here".
- **A gated instance in the app was not run on a device** before these notes. Still open there: the sign-in sheet does not close on its own after approval, team project pictures can show blank, setup packs from the instance are checked against the built-in key only, and an instance other than lolly.ing cannot open live collaboration.
- **Flow Chart into Design:** connectors are drawn for boxes on the open canvas, not inside an artboard.
- **Browser-free PNG.** A tool with no measured record needs the browser tier or `--allow-browser-free`.
- **Platforms.** Windows, Linux desktop and Android builds still wait for a hands-on WebGPU check on real hardware. Intel Macs are deprecated.
