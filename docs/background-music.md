# Free background music generator

Lolly's [music generator](/#/music) makes new background music for a video, a podcast, a stream, a game or a presentation, in more than 50 genres from lofi and jazz to synthwave and drum and bass, on your device. Music you make comes under the [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence (CC BY 4.0)](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/): free for any use, including commercial use, as long as you credit Lolly with the line **Music made with Lolly (https://lolly.tools), CC BY 4.0**.

Choose a genre to hear a short sample, read what the genre sounds like and open the music generator in that genre.

## Made on your device, with no AI

- **No samples, no loops, no recordings.** Lolly's composer writes every note, and instruments modelled in code play them. Even the drum breaks, the record scratches, the birds and the waves are synthesised.
- **No generative AI.** The composer is a set of rules for each genre, not a trained model, and it makes the same tune from the same genre and seed on every device.
- **Content Credentials in every file.** Each M4A or WAV you download carries a signed record that says Lolly's music generator made the music, procedurally and with no AI. The record's digital source type is algorithmic media. The record and the file's own tags also state the licence and the credit, **Music made with Lolly (https://lolly.tools), CC BY 4.0**.
- **Nothing to sign up for.** The music generator runs in your browser. Your tracks stay on your device until you share them.

## Questions

### Is the music free?

Yes. The music generator costs nothing, and music you make is free for any use, as long as you credit Lolly.

### What licence is the music under?

The Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence, [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The licence covers music you make in the music generator, the samples on these pages and the tunes the Neurospicy player's stations compose. The licence asks for credit: use the line **Music made with Lolly (https://lolly.tools), CC BY 4.0**. Every file you download states the licence and the credit in its tags and its Content Credentials.

### Can I use the music commercially?

Yes. You can use music you make in a paid product, an advert, a monetised video or a client project, as long as you credit Lolly.

### Do I need to credit Lolly?

Yes. The CC BY 4.0 licence asks for credit. Use the line **Music made with Lolly (https://lolly.tools), CC BY 4.0**.

### Is the music copyright-free?

No. The music comes under CC BY 4.0, a licence that asks for the credit. The music is free to use, even commercially, and royalty-free as long as you include the credit.

### Is the music made with AI?

No. Lolly's composer follows rules written for each genre and plays the notes on instruments modelled in code. No trained model writes or plays anything. Every downloaded file records the music as algorithmic media in its Content Credentials, so a checker such as [Lolly Verify](/#/verify) does not flag the music as AI.

### Does the music use samples or loops?

No. Every sound is synthesised by Lolly's own code as the track plays: the drums, the voices, the DJ's scratches, the birds and the waves included. No recording of anyone's music is in a track.

### What file types can I download?

M4A (AAC), a small file that plays almost anywhere, and WAV, an uncompressed file for editing and mastering. Each file carries Content Credentials and states its licence and credit in its tags. To remove them, run the file through the [Lolly Strip Hidden Data](/t/strip-data) utility. Removing them does not remove the credit the licence asks for.

### Can I make a longer track or a loop?

Yes. Tune sets the length of the track in bars, and a track ends with its own outro. Save the track to your assets and pick the tune as a video's music bed in Lolly; the tune loops to fill the video.

### Which browsers does the music generator work in?

Current Chrome and Edge, Safari 26 or later, and Firefox with WebGPU turned on. [Supported environments](/info/supported-environments.html) has the details. The samples on these pages play in any browser.
