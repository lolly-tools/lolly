# Lolly Design: the free canvas

<a id="design-the-free-canvas"></a>

Lolly Design is the tool for layouts no template covers: a poster from scratch, a deck of slides or a motion piece with a soundtrack. You drag, resize, rotate and type into boxes on a canvas, and the canvas exports through the same render path as every other tool.

[Using Lolly](/info/using.html) covers what every tool shares, such as saving and sharing.

## The Design window

The **top bar** starts with **Home**, the **File menu** and the document's name (click the name to rename the document). The middle holds **Undo**, **Redo**, **History**, the zoom controls (with **Fit all** and **Fit artboard**) and toggles for the **Timeline**, the **Navigator** and the **Inspector**. On the right sit **Share**, **Present** and **Export**. On a phone, the less-used buttons move into **More actions**.

The **tool rail** down the side holds the Lolly menu and the tools you draw with: **Pointer**, **Add a box**, **Pen** (the **Pencil** shares its button), **Edit points**, **Line**, then the Timeline, Artboards and Auto-arrange buttons.

![The free-canvas tool rail: a drag grip, the Lolly menu, then Pointer, Add a box, Pen, Edit points, Line, Timeline, Artboards and Auto-arrange](/t/url-shot?url=%2F%23%2Ftool%2Fdesign%3Ftemplate%3D__blank__&width=1440&height=900&dpi=192&waitMs=2600&css=.fc-toolbar%7Bopacity%3A1!important%7D&cropSelector=.fc-toolbar&walker=1&format=svg&dark=1&filename=pen-editor-rail)

The canvas works like a designer's pasteboard around the artboard:

- **Park boxes off the artboard.** A box dragged past the frame edge stays visible and selectable. Everything outside the frame is faded, and the frame's shadow marks where the file begins.
- **Only the artboard exports.** Anything outside the frame, including the part of a box over the edge, is cropped from raster and vector output.
- **Zoom out past Fit** to see everything you have parked.
- **Resize the artboard** by changing the export size. Boxes keep their positions, so you can reframe a layout around existing content. Boxes keep their positions.

### The Navigator and the Inspector

The **Navigator** lists every artboard (headed **Artboards**, or **Slides** in a deck) as a folding group of its **Layers**. Select an artboard's name to jump there, or expand the artboard to select or reorder its layers. The arrow keys move through the list, and Left returns to the artboard heading. **Pages** groups artboards into named pages. Choosing a page only changes which artboards the editor shows.

The **Inspector** puts the selected object's text or image controls first. Option chips cover the quick choices, and **Advanced** holds the styling detail. On a phone the Inspector and the layers list share one panel that drops down from under the top bar, as in every other tool. **Inspect** and **Layer/Page** in the bottom bar open the panel on their tab. Drag or tap the handle on the panel's lower edge to move the panel between a strip with the two tabs, half the screen and nearly all of the screen. At half, the canvas below the panel shows your selection. Escape folds the panel back to the strip and keeps your selection.

## Resize, scale and rotate

Select an object, then drag a handle to change its size, or drag the round handle below the selection to turn the object. A size drag works in one of two modes:

- **Resize**, the default, changes the box only. Text rewraps to the new size, and font size, stroke width, corner radius and effects stay as they were.
- **Scale** grows or shrinks the object with its content: font size, stroke, corner radius, shadow, blur, padding and spacing all change by the same amount.

Press `K` to switch Scale on or off. Without a keyboard, choose **Scale mode** in the Lolly menu, or press and hold (or right-click) the **Pointer** button on the rail and choose **Scale**. On a touch screen the object bar also has a Scale toggle. While Scale is on, the Pointer button shows the Scale icon and each handle has a dot in its middle. `V` or **Escape** returns to Resize and keeps your selection. Every document opens with Scale off, so nobody scales strokes by accident. Every document opens with Scale off.

**Scale by a number.** With Scale on, or with several objects selected, click the readout on the object bar to open the **Scale** popover. Type a percentage, a width or a height, pick the point that stays still on the 3 x 3 grid and choose **Apply**. Each scale is one undo step.

**Several objects.** A selection of several objects has handles on its edges and its corners. While every object is upright or turned by whole quarter turns, and the selection holds no group or circle, a corner drag changes width and height freely. Otherwise the selection keeps its proportions and the readout adds **Proportional**. While you drag, a small label shows the size, or the percentage in Scale mode.

**Flip a selection.** Right-click and choose **Flip horizontal** or **Flip vertical**, or press `Shift+H` or `Shift+V`. Exported SVG, PDF and PNG files keep the flip.

**Keys you hold during a drag** take effect as you press or release them. The shortcut sheet (`?`) also lists them.

| Hold | Resizing or scaling | Rotating | Moving |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shift** | Keeps the proportions (Scale always does) | Turns in 15 degree steps | No change |
| **Alt** (Option) | Works from the centre | Turns freely | Turns snapping and the grid off |
| **Cmd/Ctrl** | Turns snapping and the grid off | Turns freely | Turns snapping and the grid off |

With no key held, a turn snaps to the nearest 15 degrees when it comes within 4 degrees of one. Artboards do not rotate and have no rotate handle.

::: details Handles, grab zones and touch
**Grab anywhere along an edge.** Each side of a selection is a resize strip. With a mouse or a pen there is also a rotate zone just outside each corner. The pointer shows what a drag will do. A press in a strip or zone becomes a resize or a turn only once you start dragging, so a plain click still selects. A locked object has no handles, strips or zones.

**Small objects stay movable.** On an object 25 screen pixels or less across, the middle handles hide and the others move a little way out from the outline, so a press on the object moves the object. At 10 pixels or less the corner handles go too, and a thin line keeps a handle at each end. A press just beside a thin line or a tiny object, clear of its handles, moves the object.

**Large grab areas.** On a touch screen each corner zone is a quarter of the selection's width and height on screen, between 24 and 44 pixels, and each side zone covers the stretch between two corners. When the selection is too small, the zones move outside its outline. The zone under your pointer or finger is shaded. To get the same zones with a mouse or a pen, turn on **Large grab areas** in the Lolly menu. The setting stays on this device.

**Two fingers on a selection.** On a touch screen, put two fingers on a selected object: pinch to scale, twist to turn or move both fingers to move the selection. Each gesture is one undo step. With Scale on, a pinch also scales the content; with Scale off, a pinch resizes in proportion. A twist snaps to the nearest 15 degrees when close, and a two-finger move never snaps. The first finger must land on the selection and the second must follow quickly, on the selection or just outside its edge. Two fingers anywhere else zoom and pan the view, and so do two fingers on a selection that holds an artboard or a locked object, covers most of the view or runs past its edge.
:::

## Draw your own shapes

Boxes, circles and rounded frames cover most layouts. For any other shape, the rail's **Pen** button (or `P`) starts drawing mode. Four keys switch modes: `V` for the Pointer, `P` for the Pen, `Shift C` for the **Pencil** and `N` for **Edit points**. The Pointer always leaves drawing mode.

- **Click** to place a point. On the default curve type, **click and drag** pulls out the point's handles to make a curve; hold **Alt** as you click for a hard corner. On the other curve types every point is a corner and a drag does nothing.
- Hold **Shift** as you click to set the new segment at a multiple of 15 degrees from the point before, for level, upright and 45 degree lines. Shift also steps a handle you drag out.
- As you place them, points snap to the artboard, your other boxes and the path's other points. Alt turns off the grid while you draw, and turns off the grid and the edges when you drag a point later.
- **Click your first point** to close the shape. To keep an open drawing, press **Enter**, double-click or switch tools.
- **Escape** first abandons the drawing, then leaves the pen.
- **Delete**, or Undo (`⌘ Z` or `Ctrl Z`), while drawing drops the last point.

**Carry on an open path.** With the Pen on, press either end of an open path to continue drawing that path. Each click adds a point after that end, or before the first point when you started from the beginning. Enter keeps the result and Escape leaves the path as it was. The path stays the same box with its fill, stroke and arrowheads, and one undo reverses the change. Delete removes only the points you added. A closed shape, a line attached to a box and a path with several parts have no free end.

**Draw freehand with the Pencil.** Press `Shift C`, or press and hold (or right-click) the **Pen** button on the rail and choose **Pencil**. The button shows the Pencil until you pick the Pen again. The Pencil records your stroke as you drag. When you let go, Lolly smooths the stroke into a few points with Bezier handles. Sharp turns stay corners, and the curve stays within a couple of screen pixels of your stroke. A stroke that ends near its start closes.

A Pen or Pencil path is an ordinary box: move, resize, rotate, group, align, restack, fill, shade or fade the path like any other. The first path takes your brand's fill and stroke for paths, and each later path takes whatever you last used. When the brand sets nothing for paths, a new path is stroked in the colour you drew with, so the shape is never invisible.

**Edit the points again.** Double-click the shape, or use **Edit points** on the object bar. Drag a point to move it, drag a handle to re-aim the curve, click on the curve to insert a point or drag a box round several points and press Delete. A path always keeps at least two points.

- Hold **Shift** while you drag a point to move its angle in 15 degree steps, measured from the next point along the path that is not moving. Shift while you drag a handle steps the handle round its own point. A Shift-click still adds a point or a handle to the selection; the steps start once you drag.
- A dragged point snaps to the artboard, other boxes and the path's other points. Hold Alt to place the point freely.
- Click a handle, then press **Delete** to pull the handle back onto its point. That side of the point becomes a corner. With points selected, Delete removes the points.

**Spline type** decides what kind of curve runs through your points:

| Type | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Smooth (auto)** | The default. Sets its own handle lengths, so plain clicks give a smooth curve. A handle you set fixes the direction; the curve still sets the length. |
| **Bezier handles** | You control every handle, and inserting a point never moves the curve. |
| **Through the points** | Passes through every point, with no handles. |
| **B-spline** | Passes near the points, not through them, for a softer line. |
| **Straight lines** | A polyline. |

Switching an existing path to a type that sets its own handles asks first, because the handle lengths you set are lost. Mid-drawing the switch applies straight away, and any handles you had pulled are dropped. Switching to **Bezier handles** loses nothing. While drawing, the switch applies at once and drops any handles you pulled.

Each point also carries a continuity rule, shown by its shape: square for **Corner** (handles move independently), round for **Smooth** (handles stay in line) and round with a ring for **Symmetric** (in line and equal length). Set the rule for selected points and the curve updates at once.

## Combine shapes

Select two or more shapes, right-click the canvas (two-finger tap on touch) and choose:

- **Union** merges them into one shape with the topmost one's paint.
- **Subtract** cuts everything above away from the bottom shape.
- **Intersect** keeps only the overlap.
- **Exclude** keeps everything except the overlap.

Three more work on a single shape. **Outline stroke…** turns a stroke into a filled outline of the same weight. **Offset path…** grows the outline outwards or, with a negative number, shrinks it inwards. **Simplify** rebuilds a path with fewer segments at the same outline.

## Trace an image

Select one image, right-click it and choose **Trace image…** to turn the picture into editable shapes. Pick a preset and press **Trace**:

- **Logo** finds a few flat colours and keeps crisp edges and true circles and lines. Logo refuses a picture with gradients or photo tones and says why.
- **Illustration** traces several colours as filled shapes, flat art or a posterised picture.
- **Line art** traces dark lines on a light page as black shapes.
- **Centreline** traces dark lines as single strokes whose weight you can change.

The shapes are added as a group above the image, and the image stays in place. Hide or delete the image to see only the paths. The trace runs on your device in a background job with a Cancel button, and nothing is uploaded. A 1500 pixel image usually takes one to several seconds; a busy photograph takes longer.

The trace follows what the box shows, so a cropped (Cover) image traces only the visible part. These limits apply:

- Images are scaled down to at most 1500 pixels on the long side before tracing, so edges are placed to a fraction of that size, not of the original.
- One trace makes at most 1,500 shapes and 60,000 points. A photograph usually goes past that limit; Lolly Design then says so and changes nothing. Try Logo or Line art, or a simpler picture.
- Gradients become bands of flat colour, and soft shadows become hard-edged shapes.
- Corner radius, a shape mask and an effect on the image box are not applied to the paths.
- The same picture and preset always give the same shapes.

## Strokes

A box, a picture, a board and a pen path can each carry a stroke: a colour, a width and a style (solid, dashed or dotted). The stroke rows sit under **Fill** in the Inspector, and the **Stroke** button on the object bar opens them.

**Position** places the stroke on the outline: **Inside**, **Centre** or **Outside**. A box is stroked inside by default, so the box keeps the outer edge you drew. A pen path is stroked centred on its curve. With a centred or outside stroke, the fill, picture and text stay inside the stroke's inner edge, and the selection outline stays on the box's edge. An open path has no inside, so it is always stroked centred. A board's stroke always stays inside the page, because the page edge is the export edge.

**Stroke per side.** On a rectangle, a rounded box, a pill or a board, the toggle beside the width shows four widths: **Top**, **Right**, **Bottom** and **Left**. Set one side to 0 to hide that side, for a divider or an underline without a second shape. The toggle only shows or hides the four cells and never changes a value. While the sides differ, the width reads **Mixed**; type a width there to set all four sides. Every side shares the colour, position and style. Rounded corners curve with the widths beside them, as a CSS border does. Ellipses, circles and pen paths draw one width all round.

::: details How each format carries a stroke
| Format | Position | Per side |
|---|---|---|
| SVG, PDF, PNG and the other pictures | Exact. A pen path's inside or outside stroke is drawn twice as wide and clipped to one side of the outline. | A solid stroke is exact, as one shape between the stroke's outer and inner edges. A dashed or dotted one is drawn as dashes along each side in SVG and solid in PDF. |
| Penpot | Inside, centre and outside travel as Penpot's stroke alignment. A board is always `inner`. | Penpot 2.19 or later reads the four widths natively. Penpot 2.18 draws the top width all round, and Penpot 2.17 or earlier refuses a file that carries widths per side. |
| PowerPoint | PowerPoint draws a line centred on the shape's edge. Centre is exact. Outside moves the shape out by half the width, so PowerPoint's line is outside the box. Inside keeps the centred line older exports wrote. | The stroke becomes a separate shape (the ring between the outer and inner edges), over a shape with no line. |
| dotLottie | Lottie strokes are centred, so an outside box stroke or a pen path stroked inside or outside is refused with a message. | Refused with a message. |
:::

A translucent centred stroke shows the page through its inner half, not the fill, on the canvas and in every export. An imported Penpot file keeps each stroke's alignment and widths per side. A Figma file's `strokeAlign` and individual stroke weights come across too.

## 3D scenes and tools inside a design

A box can hold more than a picture. A 3D scene or a tool keeps a recipe, not a rendered image, so the box redraws at any size and moment the document asks for. An update to the source reaches every design that uses the source.

### 3D scenes

Choose **3D scene** from the rail's add menu and drag out a frame. Lolly 3D Studio opens on the new box, and your settings there return to the canvas. **Edit in 3D Studio** in the Inspector's **3D scene** section reopens the studio. [Place a scene in Lolly Design](/info/3d-studio.html#place-a-scene-in-lolly-design) covers posters, the timeline, export and sharing.

### Tools inside a design

Choose **Image** from the rail's add menu, drag out a frame and pick a tool on the picker's **Tools** tab. The tool fills the frame with its defaults, and the Inspector opens on a **Tool** section holding the tool's controls. Change a field and the box updates a moment later, as one undo step. **Image fit** and **Image position** work as for any picture, and **Replace with another tool or image** swaps the box's content.

**Tools that move** play inside the box. A looping illustration loops on the canvas, follows the playhead once the timeline is open and exports frame by frame to GIF, MP4 or WebM. When the tool states its loop length, the box gets a clip of that length, so a video holds whole loops. A clip you retimed keeps your timing. Turning the tool's motion off makes the box a still picture.

## Timeline (Sequence)

**Sequence** is Lolly Design's timeline, docked under the artboard. Every box can start at a moment, run for a length and animate in and out.

Lolly Design's **Video** template starts with a short sequence: two timed text cards on the sequence row and a procedural music bed under them.

There are two kinds of row:

- The **sequence row** is magnetic. Clips sit end to end, dragging one reorders the run and deleting one closes the gap. This is the spine.
- **Overlay lanes** are free. A lower-third, a logo or a caption floats over the spine at its own time, each in its own lane.
- Below them, **Always on** holds boxes with no timing, shown throughout. The `+` on a chip moves one onto a lane; **Make always on** sends it back.

While the timeline is open, Space and the arrow keys drive the playhead. A composition that already has timing opens the timeline automatically.

**Editing.** Drag a clip's middle to move it, drag near either end to trim it and press **Split at playhead** (or `S`) to cut it in two. Split needs a clip with a set **Length** and the playhead a little way inside the clip, so an open-ended clip such as the music bed cannot be split. **Snap to edges** is on by default and snaps to clip edges, the playhead and whole seconds. Hold Alt to drag without snapping. Every drag is one undo step, and the drag preview matches the result.

Select a clip and the inspector gives the same edits as numbers: **Length**, **Trim in** (how far into the source the clip starts), **Speed** from ×0.25 to ×4, **Animate in** and **Animate out** with their lengths and **Mute clip**. A clip on the magnetic row has no **Start** field, because the row sets the order.

**Transitions** are presets: Fade, Pop, Grow, Rise, Drop, the four Slides, Zoom in and out, Tilt, Swoop, Spin, Drift or **Cut (no animation)**. Distances scale with the object, so one preset works on a full-frame card and on a small badge. Between two clips on the sequence row, a **seam chip** chooses **Cut** or **Crossfade**. Open the chip again to set the **Length (ms)**, then press **Done**. In a crossfade, the first clip plays past the cut and fades out while the next fades in, in the preview and in the file.

**Sound.** An **Audio** clip is on the timeline with a waveform, trim and mute. The Video template's generated music bed is synthesised at export time, so its bar stays plain and silent until you render. Press the microphone to **record a voiceover** onto the timeline, with a count-in and a level meter. The take is saved as your own asset at the playhead. The camera button beside the microphone **records a video** the same way, cropped to the artboard, so a colleague can add a clip from a shared link. Select a clip with sound to open its strip under the timeline for volume, pan, EQ, pitch, normalising and effects such as **Voice cleanup**. [Sound](/info/sequence-editor.html#sound) in the sequence editor guide covers the strip. The export panel's **Audio track** is separate: one bed under the whole clip, with fade and ducking.

**Rendering.** A motion export draws, decodes and encodes each frame at an exact time, so the file does not depend on your machine's speed. MP4 and WebM have no practical frame limit. The timeline's length sets the duration unless you type one. Content Credentials are stamped as on any other export. A still export gives the frame at the playhead, or a contact sheet from the **Frames** field; see [Stills from a timed composition](/info/exporting.html#stills-from-a-timed-composition). A sequence is capped at one hour. GIF and animated PNG hold every frame in memory, so they suit short clips. **Record live** is hidden here in favour of the frame-by-frame render.

**Keyframes, depth and a camera.** A transition animates a clip as the clip arrives and leaves. To pose a box within a clip (drift, fade, blur, lift it off the page and settle it back), select the clip and press **+Keyframe**: the diamond in the timeline's tools, the diamond on the canvas object bar or **Shift+K** with the timeline focused. The playhead's position decides which pose your next edit writes. The same keyframes give a timed composition a camera that pushes in, pans and pulls focus. An ease can be a [spring](/info/animating.html#springs) or a curve your design system defines, and dragging the handles on a position keyframe curves the [motion path](/info/animating.html#edit-the-motion-path) on the canvas. [Animating](/info/animating.html) is the full guide, and [The sequence editor](/info/sequence-editor.html) covers selection, onion skin, splitting, trimming and markers.

## Before you export

The **Before you export** card checks your document before you send the file. The card appears in the Inspector's **Document** section and in the export panel.

The card reads the saved layer structure, then checks the settled canvas for clipped text and flat-colour contrast. The card asks the font registry used for SVG and PDF outlining whether each text run has font bytes that can be embedded. Text that a [fallback font](/info/fallback-fonts.html) draws in the export counts as covered. A script with no fallback font, or a Chinese, Japanese or Korean character outside the sets those fonts hold, is still reported. Image and gradient backgrounds are listed as visual checks, with no made-up contrast score.

### Check a composition against the design system

The export panel's card also compares the composition with your design system, at the same design-system version the render uses. It checks authored colours, token aliases, font choices and image asset ids. A custom value may be intentional, and an image outside the declared brand assets is a review item, not a banned image.

Where a colour or font suggestion exists, its button changes that one layer, and **Undo** restores the original. An old suggestion never overwrites a locked or since-changed layer. The comparison leaves out gradients, effects, nested tool content, rights and subjective quality. No check blocks Download.

### House rules and the writing signal

The export panel's card also checks, layer by layer, the house rules in your design system, such as text weight and alignment, approved colour pairings or a logo per surface. Each finding shows the layer's name above the rule. A finding against a required rule is a warning, and one against an advisory rule is a note. A logo finding gives each logo's name from the catalog. A rule this build has no checker for is listed as not checked, never as followed. [Rules](/info/brand-studio.html#rules) lists the rules, and `lolly check` applies the same ones to a saved document. In a Lolly Work partner programme, the card also checks a partner company's marks against its [partner kit](/info/manage/partner-programmes.html#partner-kits), and says what it could not measure.

The same card shows the writing signal for the text the document draws: its band (none, weak, notable or strong), then Lolly Verify's sentence with the score out of 100 and up to three flagged phrases, each with the layer that holds them. **Open the text in Verify** takes that text to [Lolly Verify](/#/verify) for the full reading. [AI evidence in Lolly Verify](/info/verify-ai-evidence.html) explains what the score weighs. The score is a signal, not a verdict. The score counts patterns common in generated writing and cannot tell who wrote the words. Hidden layers are left out of both checks, and the card says how many.

Findings against required house rules and a strong writing signal count toward the card's "to fix" total. Advisory findings count as things to know. Findings never block: Download stays available, and there is nothing to dismiss or record as an exception. An agent working in the open document gets the same report through its site tools ([Agents in an open document](/info/open-document-agents.html)).

::: note Print checks need a feature flag
The print checks (bleed, resolution, ink coverage and plate counts) join the same card when you turn on **Settings → Feature flags → Print preflight**. In other tools, the card appears only with that flag on. [Export a file](/info/exporting.html) describes the print checks.
:::

## Presenting

A Lolly Design document made of artboards is a deck. Press **Present** in the top bar and each artboard becomes a full-screen slide, in the order the artboards sit on the canvas.

The button reads **Preview** until the document's **Design outcome** is **Slides**. The deck runs on a copy of the rendered artboards, so leaving returns you to where you were.

The arrow beside **Present** opens **Present from this slide**, **Speaker view**, **Present with camera**, **Narrate**, **Auto-advance slides**, **Loop the deck (kiosk)** and **Export slides as video**.

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Space**, `→`, **Page Down** | Next step or slide (or click the strip at the right edge) |
| `←`, **Page Up** | Back (or click the strip at the left edge) |
| **Home**, **End** | First or last slide |
| `O` | Overview: every artboard in your canvas arrangement; click one to open it |
| `S` | Speaker view: current slide, next slide, notes and a clock in a second window |
| `B` | Black screen; any key brings the slide back |
| `F` | Back to fullscreen |
| `K` | Pause or restart a kiosk deck |
| **Escape** | Go back one level: overview to deck, deck to editor |

A small control bar appears when you move the pointer and hides when you stop. If the browser blocks the speaker pop-up, speaker view opens as a panel over the deck. Notes belong to each artboard and never appear on the slide.

On a touch screen, swipe left for the next step or slide and right to go back. A **Slide** or **Fade** transition follows your finger and finishes when you let go. A **Cut**, **Morph** or **Fly between artboards** transition plays in full when you let go. A short, slow swipe changes nothing. Web page boxes, video controls, boxes that scroll and selected text keep their own gestures, so a swipe never starts on them. Vertical scrolling and pinch zoom work as usual. With reduced motion the slide still changes but does not follow your finger.

- **Reveal steps.** Right-click a box and choose **Reveal at step 1**, **2** or **3** instead of **Always visible**. The box appears when you advance to its step. Boxes sharing a number arrive together.
- **Kiosk.** Give an artboard a **Length** and the deck holds there for that long, then advances, with a thin progress bar. Add `kiosk` to the link to loop the deck at the end, for signage.
- **Sub-slide stacks.** Right-click an artboard and choose **Stack under the previous slide** to make it a step of that slide. The overview shows one card, and the inspector's **Stack** row says which slide the artboard belongs to.
- **Morph.** When consecutive slides both hold a box with the same **Morph match** name (such as `hero`), the transition moves, resizes and recolours that box instead of cutting. A deck-wide **Morph** transition does the same for every matched pair.
- **Narration.** Each artboard's **Speaker notes** can be read aloud. In the Inspector's **Document** section choose a **Voice**, optionally a second voice to **Blend with**, the **Speed** and a **Lead-in** and **Tail** in milliseconds. **Show captions when presenting** shows the words as they are spoken. The voice runs on your device. The same notes become the soundtrack of a video export, slide audio in a PowerPoint export and the narrated film in a [SCORM package](/info/create/exporting.html#scorm-course-packages).

The deck is also a link. `?present` opens the deck and `s=` picks the slide: a position, an artboard id or `id.step` for a build step. The address follows the current slide, so the link you send opens on that slide. [Reserved URL parameters](/info/url-parameters.html#what-happens-on-open) documents both.

**Present with camera** places your camera, a logo and a name caption over the slides, with private controls and saved scenes; see [Presenting with camera](/info/presenting.html). A **Web page** box can take the clicker during a talk when you turn on **Make interactive** in its inspector. At its **Focus on click** step, Up and Down control the page while Next continues the deck. [Live pages that take the clicker](/info/interactive-pages.html) covers focus styles, scroll stops and site approval.
